**Submitted by:** [Ali abdulmalik] — Academy: @SDAIAAcademy  
**Dataset:** Monthly International Air Passengers (1949–1960, 144 observations, no gaps)

## 1. The Recommendation
* **Recommendation:** We recommend shipping the **AutoARIMA** model for our monthly passenger forecasting pipeline.
* **The Supporting Number:** AutoARIMA outperformed the seasonal naive benchmark across our rolling-origin cross-validation harness, capturing the upward trend and changing seasonal swing magnitudes much more effectively than the baseline.

## 2. The Intervals
* **Band Width & Coverage:** The prediction intervals generated by the harness (ranging from 20% up to 95% levels) provided a robust view of uncertainty. 
* **Honesty Check:** The 80% and 95% coverage metrics indicate that the bands adequately capture the historical volatility and structural growth of passenger numbers as the scale of the airline market expands, without being overly conservative or falsely narrow.

## 3. The Residuals
* **What was missed:** The residual analysis of our model shows that while AutoARIMA successfully captures the main trend and seasonal components, minor variance remains at specific high-growth holiday peaks.
* **Implication:** The residual ACF plot confirms that most linear structures have been eaten, leaving near white-noise behavior, though unusual economic or external shocks in specific months still require monitoring.

## 4. One Change (Next Step)
* **Specific Next Step:** Implement **Dynamic Regression** incorporating an exogenous driver, such as monthly mean regional temperatures or macroeconomic travel indices (e.g., fetched via public weather/economic archives like Open-Meteo), cached as a CSV.
* **Expected Outcome:** This addition will help explain the residual variance tied to external environmental or seasonal drivers, further tightening the point and interval forecast accuracy.
